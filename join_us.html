Read the full lab introduction

I am a University-appointed Associate Research Fellow and graduate supervisor at the School of Artificial Intelligence, Hubei University, and the founder of the Embodied AI Lab.

Our group develops algorithms at the frontiers of artificial intelligence and explores AI in the physical world. We want to help AI move from digital environments into real-world applications.

  • Research directions
    • VLA Multimodal Models: Vision-Language-Action models that give robots cognitive capabilities.
    • Robot Control: Control in complex environments and dexterous manipulation for quadruped robots and robotic arms.
    • Reinforcement Learning: Autonomous learning and intelligent decision-making in dynamic environments.
    • Computer Vision: Perception in complex environments and understanding three-dimensional space.
    • Differential Privacy: Building secure and trustworthy machine-learning systems.
  • Why this research matters

    Bringing intelligent agents from digital environments into the physical world is widely considered a key milestone toward artificial general intelligence (AGI). Rapid advances in deep learning and computer vision have given AI powerful perceptual capabilities. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have added capabilities resembling human cognition, reasoning and common-sense understanding. Turning these brain-like abilities into practical actions in the physical world—embodied AI—remains a frontier challenge.

    By combining reinforcement learning with Vision-Language-Action models, we aim to bridge perception, cognition and control, enabling robot dogs and robotic arms to adapt autonomously to complex environments. As AI systems interact more closely with society, protecting privacy during training on large volumes of multimodal data becomes increasingly important. We therefore also investigate differential privacy to build embodied AI systems that are intelligent, secure and trustworthy.

    Many scientific questions remain open. For example:

    • How can efficient VLA models align visual perception, language instructions and robot actions end to end?
    • How can reinforcement learning enable robust control and dexterous manipulation for quadruped robots and robotic arms in highly uncertain physical environments?
    • How can we reduce or eliminate the Sim2Real gap between simulated training and real-world deployment?
    • How can advanced computer vision improve fine-grained spatial perception in dynamic, complex three-dimensional environments?
    • How can differential privacy prevent sensitive-data leakage when training large multimodal and embodied-control models?
    • How can robot systems continually learn and improve from very few human demonstrations or real-world trials?

    Developing capable, generalizable and secure embodied AI requires more researchers with potential and enthusiasm to join this field.

    This emerging field is receiving attention from national strategies, local governments and society, and is viewed as the next wave of AI. At a broad level, AI with physical execution and multimodal perception could transform manufacturing and everyday life. At an individual level, skills in deep learning, reinforcement learning, VLA models, robot control and privacy-preserving computation can support a competitive career. Major internet companies, technology firms, and autonomous-driving and robotics companies are actively recruiting embodied-AI researchers and engineers, with a broad range of opportunities and attractive compensation.

Read the full training plan

Our approach combines sustained mentoring, systematic training and hands-on work. We support students from their first research steps to independent work, combining structured training with individual guidance to develop research, academic communication and engineering skills.

  • Regular academic exchange: building research perspective and critical thinking
    • Weekly group meetings: we study recent papers from leading conferences and journals. The supervisor guides close reading of core contributions, experimental design, writing and research methods, encouraging discussion and critical thinking.
    • Focused discussions and progress reports: regular research updates, literature reviews and project proposals help students organize their thinking and develop presentation, analytical and critical-questioning skills.
  • Individual supervision: addressing research challenges
    • At least one dedicated discussion each week: individual guidance covers research direction, obstacles, experiments and writing, from topic selection and study design to detailed refinements.
    • Support throughout the process: literature review, research planning, experimental improvement, data processing, analysis, manuscript revision, submission and responses to reviewers.
  • Academic support: broadening opportunities for exchange
    • Support for domestic and international conferences: students are encouraged to attend respected conferences, with guidance on participation and academic exchange, to meet researchers and present their work.
    • Publication practice and academic standards: training covers submission procedures, choosing journals and conferences, and responding to reviews.
  • Connecting research and industry: practical experience
    • Internship recommendations: connections with companies and research institutes help us recommend suitable opportunities, connecting research with engineering practice and industry projects.
    • Collaborative applied projects: students are encouraged to join externally funded and real-world projects that develop practical problem-solving skills and connect research with industry needs.
  • Training across the research process
    • Research design: literature search and review, defining questions, identifying contributions, designing experiments, and selecting controls and comparisons.
    • Experimental methods: systematic data collection, processing, analysis and visualization; professional tools, replication and verification; and rigorous, reproducible research habits.
    • Academic writing: guidance on structure, abstracts, argumentation, figures and language, with focused practice in English writing, academic standards and responses to reviewers.

Our focus is developing ability, beyond completing projects or publishing papers. We want students to independently identify problems, design studies, solve problems and communicate results, building a foundation for further study, academic research or other careers.

Read the full support details

The group aims to provide a productive research environment, research funding and a supportive team:

  • Hardware and infrastructure
    • Personal equipment: support for a high-performance desktop or laptop, a productivity monitor, and peripherals such as a keyboard, mouse and headphones, depending on circumstances.
    • Experimental and computing resources: free or convenient access to group equipment; support for the use of shared facilities; selected licensed software such as WPS; and server, NAS and other storage capacity.
    • Workspace: a dedicated desk and workstation, a safe and equipped laboratory, high-speed networking, and printing and photocopying allowances.
  • Research funding
    • Consumables: group funding covers research materials, consumables, fabrication and relevant sample-analysis costs.
    • Publication costs: support for open-access publication fees (for example, USD 3,500) and relevant illustration or typesetting tools such as Origin and Illustrator.
  • Travel and conference support
    • Academic conferences: support for registration fees (USD 800–1,200), return transport and accommodation; students are encouraged to present talks or posters.
    • Academic exchange and visits: support for short research visits to domestic or international collaborators and travel for fieldwork or company visits when required.
  • Skills and professional development
    • Skills training: support for selected paid research-related courses, short courses and summer schools, with internship opportunities based on performance.
    • Supervision and academic networks: regular individual guidance, introductions to researchers, and group meetings and literature discussions.
  • Community and recognition
    • Additional rewards: performance bonuses for strong publications, patents or awards, subject to the group's financial circumstances.
    • Wellbeing and support: team meals and other activities, and care and assistance when members face difficulties such as illness.
  • Support for academic training and summer schools.
